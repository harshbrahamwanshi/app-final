# Academic Regulations Handbook
### Institute of Technology and Applied Sciences
### Effective Academic Year 2026–27

---

## 1. Preliminary

### 1.1 Applicability
1.1.1 These regulations govern all students enrolled in undergraduate and postgraduate programmes of the Institute, unless a specific school or department has notified an approved deviation to the Office of Academic Affairs.

1.1.2 In the event of any conflict between these regulations and a school-specific handbook, these regulations shall prevail unless the school-specific handbook has been explicitly ratified by the Academic Council as a superseding document.

1.1.3 Where these regulations are silent on a matter, the Dean of Academic Affairs may issue a written clarification, which shall be binding once circulated to the relevant department.

### 1.2 Definitions
1.2.1 "Academic Term" means a semester of approximately ninety (90) instructional days, excluding examination periods.

1.2.2 "Competent Authority" means the specific committee, officer, or body identified in a given clause as having power to decide that matter.

1.2.3 "Working Day" means a day on which the Institute's administrative offices are open, excluding Sundays and notified holidays.

1.2.4 "Result Declaration Date" means the date on which the Controller of Examinations formally publishes a result on the student portal.

---

## 2. Registration and Enrollment

### 2.1 Semester Registration
2.1.1 Every student must complete semester registration within the first ten (10) working days of the term.

2.1.2 A student who fails to register within this window shall be marked "Not Registered" and shall not be permitted to attend classes or sit for any assessment in that term, subject to clause 2.2.

### 2.2 Late Registration
2.2.1 The Head of Department may permit late registration up to twenty (20) working days into the term, on payment of a late registration fee as specified in the Fee Schedule.

2.2.2 No registration shall be permitted after the twentieth working day except by written order of the Dean of Academic Affairs in cases of documented hardship.

### 2.3 Course Withdrawal
2.3.1 A student may withdraw from a registered course within thirty (30) calendar days of the start of the term without academic penalty.

2.3.2 Withdrawal after this period but before the commencement of end-term examinations shall be recorded as "Withdrawn" on the transcript and shall not affect the student's Cumulative Grade Point Average.

---

## 3. Attendance Requirements

### 3.1 General Attendance Norm
3.1.1 Every student is expected to maintain regular attendance in all registered courses, including lectures, tutorials, laboratory sessions, and any other scheduled instructional activity.

3.1.2 Attendance shall be recorded by the course instructor or an authorized teaching assistant for every scheduled session and shall be uploaded to the student portal within three (3) working days of that session.

### 3.2 Minimum Attendance to Sit for Examinations
3.2.1 A student must have a minimum of seventy-five percent (75%) attendance, computed across all scheduled sessions of a course in a given term, to be eligible to sit for the end-term examination of that course.

3.2.2 Under no circumstances shall a student with attendance below seventy-five percent (75%) in a course be permitted to appear for the end-term examination of that course. This threshold is absolute and applies uniformly to every student and every course offered by the Institute.

3.2.3 A student who is ineligible under clause 3.2.2 shall be assigned a grade of "I" (Ineligible) for that course, which shall be treated as equivalent to a fail for the purpose of computing the Cumulative Grade Point Average, and shall be required to repeat the course when next offered.

### 3.3 Condonation of Shortage
3.3.1 A student whose attendance falls between sixty percent (60%) and seventy-five percent (75%) may apply to the Departmental Attendance Committee for condonation of the shortage, subject to payment of a condonation fee as specified in the Fee Schedule.

3.3.2 Condonation under clause 3.3.1 may be granted only on documented grounds such as participation in Institute-sanctioned sporting, cultural, or academic representation events, and shall not be granted for reasons of personal convenience.

3.3.3 The Departmental Attendance Committee's decision on condonation is final and is not subject to appeal, save as provided in clause 3.6.

### 3.4 Attendance and Illness
3.4.1 A student who is unable to attend classes due to illness must submit medical documentation to the Department Office within seven (7) calendar days of resuming attendance.

3.4.2 Medical documentation must be issued by a registered medical practitioner and must state the dates of incapacity.

### 3.5 Medical Exemption From the Attendance Threshold
3.5.1 A student who has been hospitalized for a period exceeding five (5) consecutive days, or who has a chronic medical condition certified by the Institute Medical Officer, may apply to the Dean of Academic Affairs for a Medical Exemption from the attendance requirement for examination eligibility.

3.5.2 Where a Medical Exemption is granted under clause 3.5.1, the student shall be deemed eligible to sit for the end-term examination provided their attendance in the affected course is not below sixty-five percent (65%), notwithstanding the threshold specified in clause 3.2.

3.5.3 An application for Medical Exemption must be submitted no later than fifteen (15) calendar days before the commencement of end-term examinations, accompanied by certification from the Institute Medical Officer.

3.5.4 The grant of a Medical Exemption under this clause does not extend to condonation fees, which remain payable in full.

### 3.6 Attendance Appeals
3.6.1 A student may appeal a denial of condonation or medical exemption to the Student Grievance Committee within five (5) working days of being notified of the denial.

3.6.2 The Student Grievance Committee shall dispose of the appeal within ten (10) working days and its decision shall be final.

## 4. Examinations

### 4.1 Eligibility
4.1.1 Eligibility to sit for end-term examinations is governed by clause 3.2, subject to any exemption validly granted under clause 3.5.

4.1.2 A student must have no outstanding fee dues, as defined in Section 8, to be permitted to sit for any examination.

### 4.2 Conduct of Examinations
4.2.1 Examinations shall be conducted as per the schedule published by the Controller of Examinations at least thirty (30) days in advance.

4.2.2 A student found engaging in unfair means during an examination shall be reported to the Disciplinary Committee under Section 9.

### 4.3 Absence From Examinations
4.3.1 A student who is unable to sit for a scheduled examination due to a medical emergency, hospitalization, or the death of an immediate family member may apply for a Supplementary Examination.

4.3.2 An application under clause 4.3.1 must be submitted within seven (7) calendar days of the missed examination, along with supporting documentation. Applications made on grounds not enumerated in clause 4.3.1 — including travel, family functions, employment interviews, or personal commitments — shall not be entertained, except where the Dean of Academic Affairs exercises discretion under clause 1.1.3.

4.3.3 Supplementary Examinations shall be held not later than thirty (30) days after the original examination, on a date notified by the Controller of Examinations.

### 4.4 Grading
4.4.1 Courses shall be graded on a ten-point scale, with a minimum passing grade of "D" (4.0 grade points) in each course.

4.4.2 A student who fails a course must repeat it either through re-registration or, where offered, a Backlog Examination.

## 5. Re-evaluation and Revaluation

### 5.1 Right to Re-evaluation
5.1.1 A student who is dissatisfied with a grade awarded in an end-term examination may apply for re-evaluation of the answer script.

5.1.2 Re-evaluation involves a re-check of totaling and a review by a second examiner of any question where the student contests the marks awarded.

### 5.2 Application Window
5.2.1 An application for re-evaluation must be submitted to the Controller of Examinations within ten (10) calendar days of the Result Declaration Date, accompanied by the prescribed fee.

5.2.2 Applications received after this window shall not be processed, and the fee, if submitted, shall not be refunded.

### 5.3 Outcome
5.3.1 The re-evaluation outcome shall be published within twenty-one (21) days of the close of the application window.

5.3.2 If the re-evaluated mark differs from the original mark by more than ten percent (10%) of the maximum marks for that examination, the re-evaluation fee shall be refunded to the student.

5.3.3 The mark obtained after re-evaluation, whether higher or lower than the original, shall be treated as final and shall replace the original mark on the transcript.

## 6. Scholarships and Financial Aid

### 6.1 Merit Scholarship
6.1.1 Students who secure a Cumulative Grade Point Average of 8.5 or above in a given academic year are eligible for a Merit Scholarship covering twenty-five percent (25%) of tuition fees for the subsequent year.

6.1.2 A student under disciplinary sanction, as recorded under Section 9, shall not be eligible for a Merit Scholarship in the year the sanction is active, regardless of their Cumulative Grade Point Average.

### 6.2 Need-Based Financial Aid
6.2.1 Students from households with an annual verified income below the threshold notified annually by the Institute's Financial Aid Office may apply for Need-Based Financial Aid.

6.2.2 Applications must be submitted through the Financial Aid Office within thirty (30) days of the start of the academic year, along with income documentation.

6.2.3 Need-Based Financial Aid, once granted, continues for the duration of the programme provided the student maintains a minimum Cumulative Grade Point Average of 6.0 and submits renewed income documentation annually.

### 6.3 Sports and Cultural Scholarship
6.3.1 Students who represent the Institute at state, national, or international level in sporting or cultural events may apply to the Sports and Cultural Committee for a scholarship covering up to fifty percent (50%) of tuition fees, at the Committee's discretion.

6.3.2 A student receiving a Sports and Cultural Scholarship remains subject to the minimum attendance requirements of Section 3, save for the condonation considerations described in clause 3.3.2.

### 6.4 Combining Scholarships
6.4.1 A student may not simultaneously hold a Merit Scholarship and a Sports and Cultural Scholarship; where eligible for both, the student must elect one.

6.4.2 Need-Based Financial Aid may be combined with either the Merit Scholarship or the Sports and Cultural Scholarship, subject to a combined cap of seventy-five percent (75%) of total tuition fees.

## 7. Hostel Accommodation

### 7.1 Allotment
7.1.1 Hostel accommodation is allotted on a first-come, first-served basis to students who submit a completed application along with the hostel deposit specified in the Fee Schedule.

7.1.2 Priority in allotment is given to first-year students and to students residing more than fifty (50) kilometers from the Institute.

### 7.2 Hostel Fees
7.2.1 Hostel fees are payable per semester in accordance with the schedule maintained separately in the Fee Schedule document.

7.2.2 A student who vacates hostel accommodation before the end of a semester is not entitled to a pro-rated refund of hostel fees, except in cases of medical withdrawal certified by the Institute Medical Officer.

### 7.3 Hostel Conduct
7.3.1 Detailed rules governing day-to-day conduct within hostel premises, including curfew timings, guest policy, and room inspection, are set out in the Hostel Handbook issued separately by the Office of Student Affairs.

7.3.2 Violations of hostel conduct rules are addressed in the first instance by the Hostel Warden. Matters the Warden considers serious, or which involve repeat violations, shall be referred to the Disciplinary Committee under Section 9.

## 8. Fees

### 8.1 Tuition and Other Fees
8.1.1 Tuition, examination, hostel, and other fees are payable in accordance with the Fee Schedule maintained in the document titled "Fee Deadlines and Penalties," which is incorporated into these regulations by reference.

8.1.2 In the event of any discrepancy between an amount stated in these regulations and the Fee Schedule document, the Fee Schedule document shall prevail, as it is updated more frequently to reflect approved fee revisions.

### 8.2 Late Payment
8.2.1 Fees paid after the due date specified in the Fee Schedule attract a late fee as specified therein.

8.2.2 A student with fee dues outstanding for more than sixty (60) days after the due date may be administratively withdrawn from the programme, subject to a final notice period of fifteen (15) days.

### 8.3 Refunds
8.3.1 Tuition fee refunds on withdrawal are governed by the schedule in the Fee Schedule document, which specifies the refund percentage applicable at different points in the term.

## 9. Discipline

### 9.1 Disciplinary Committee
9.1.1 The Institute constitutes a Disciplinary Committee comprising the Dean of Student Affairs (Chair), two faculty members nominated by the Academic Council, and one student representative in a non-voting capacity.

9.1.2 The Disciplinary Committee has final authority over all matters of student conduct across the Institute, including but not limited to academic dishonesty, examination unfair means, hostel violations, harassment complaints, and damage to Institute property, and its decisions on such matters are binding on all Institute officers.

### 9.2 Procedure
9.2.1 A matter is referred to the Disciplinary Committee by written complaint from a faculty member, administrative officer, or student.

9.2.2 The student against whom a complaint is made shall be given written notice of the allegation and an opportunity to respond in writing or in person before the Committee reaches a decision.

9.2.3 The Committee shall communicate its decision in writing within fifteen (15) working days of the matter being referred to it.

### 9.3 Sanctions
9.3.1 Sanctions available to the Disciplinary Committee include a written warning, fine, suspension from a course or hostel, suspension from the Institute for a specified period, or expulsion, depending on the severity of the matter.

9.3.2 A sanction of suspension or expulsion requires the concurrence of at least three of the four voting members of the Committee.

### 9.4 Appeals
9.4.1 A student may appeal a Disciplinary Committee decision to the Vice-Chancellor within ten (10) working days of receiving the decision.

9.4.2 The Vice-Chancellor's decision on appeal is final and no further appeal lies within the Institute.

## 10. Committee Override Powers

### 10.1 General Power
10.1.1 The Academic Council may, by resolution, waive or modify the application of any provision of these regulations in an individual case, where it is satisfied that strict application would cause manifest and disproportionate hardship to a student.

10.1.2 A waiver under clause 10.1.1 requires a written application from the student, routed through the Head of Department, and shall be considered only after all other remedies available under these regulations have been exhausted.

10.1.3 The Academic Council's decision on a waiver application is final.

### 10.2 Emergency Powers
10.2.1 In the event of circumstances beyond the Institute's control, including natural disaster, epidemic, or infrastructure failure, the Vice-Chancellor may issue emergency directions modifying any provision of these regulations for a specified period, which shall be reported to the Academic Council at its next meeting.

## 12. Transfer and Credit Recognition

### 12.1 Inter-Institutional Transfer
12.1.1 A student seeking transfer from another recognized institution may apply to the Academic Council for admission with credit for courses already completed.

12.1.2 Credit recognition is subject to a maximum of fifty percent (50%) of the programme's total credit requirement and requires a syllabus comparison conducted by the relevant department.

### 12.2 Internal Transfer
12.2.1 A student seeking to transfer between programmes within the Institute may apply to the Dean of Academic Affairs after completing at least two full academic terms in the original programme, subject to seat availability in the destination programme and a minimum Cumulative Grade Point Average of 6.5.

---

## 13. Grievance Redressal

### 13.1 Student Grievance Committee
13.1.1 The Student Grievance Committee, constituted under the Office of Student Affairs, handles complaints relating to academic evaluation, attendance disputes, harassment (other than matters referred directly to the Disciplinary Committee), and administrative grievances.

13.1.2 A grievance must be submitted in writing within thirty (30) days of the event giving rise to the grievance.

13.1.3 The Committee shall acknowledge receipt within three (3) working days and shall communicate a resolution within twenty-one (21) days, extendable once by a further fourteen (14) days for cause shown.

### 13.2 Escalation
13.2.1 A student dissatisfied with the outcome of a grievance may escalate the matter to the Dean of Academic Affairs, whose decision shall be final except in matters of academic evaluation, which may be further escalated to the Academic Council.

## 14. Academic Integrity

### 14.1 Definition of Academic Dishonesty
14.1.1 Academic dishonesty includes, but is not limited to: plagiarism in assignments, theses, or projects; unauthorized collaboration on individually assessed work; fabrication or falsification of data or results; impersonation of another student in an examination or assessment; and unauthorized use of notes, devices, or materials during an examination.

14.1.2 Plagiarism is defined as the presentation of another person's words, ideas, data, or work as one's own without appropriate acknowledgment, whether the source is published, unpublished, or another student's work.

14.1.3 Self-plagiarism, meaning the resubmission of a student's own previously submitted work for a different assessment without disclosure and prior permission of the instructor, is also treated as academic dishonesty.

### 14.2 Detection and Reporting
14.2.1 Course instructors are responsible for the first-level screening of submitted work for academic dishonesty, using similarity-detection software or other reasonable means.

14.2.2 Where an instructor has reasonable grounds to suspect academic dishonesty, the matter must be reported in writing to the Head of Department within five (5) working days of the suspicion arising.

14.2.3 The Head of Department shall conduct a preliminary review and, where warranted, refer the matter to the Disciplinary Committee under Section 9.

### 14.3 Consequences
14.3.1 A first proven instance of academic dishonesty in a piece of coursework typically results in a zero mark for that piece of work and a formal warning recorded on the student's file, at the discretion of the Disciplinary Committee.

14.3.2 A second or subsequent proven instance, or a first instance involving examination unfair means, typically results in a fail grade for the course and may result in suspension, at the discretion of the Disciplinary Committee, having regard to the severity and circumstances of the case.

14.3.3 In cases involving a thesis, dissertation, or final-year project, a proven instance of plagiarism may result in withholding of the degree pending resubmission, or, in serious cases, rescission of an already-conferred degree by resolution of the Academic Council.

## 15. Postgraduate Research and Thesis Regulations

### 15.1 Supervision
15.1.1 Every postgraduate research student shall be assigned a primary supervisor and, where the department deems appropriate, a co-supervisor, within thirty (30) days of commencing the programme.

15.1.2 A supervisor may be changed on the written request of the student or supervisor, subject to approval by the Head of Department, provided the change does not unreasonably delay the student's progress.

### 15.2 Progress Review
15.2.1 Research students shall present their progress to a Departmental Research Committee at intervals of not more than six (6) months.

15.2.2 A student who receives two consecutive "Unsatisfactory" progress ratings shall be referred to the Dean of Academic Affairs, who may recommend a revised research plan, a change of supervisor, or, in cases of sustained unsatisfactory progress, discontinuation from the programme.

### 15.3 Thesis Submission
15.3.1 A thesis must be submitted for examination only after the student has satisfied all coursework requirements and has at least one manuscript accepted or submitted to a peer-reviewed venue, where this is a programme requirement.

15.3.2 The thesis shall be examined by at least two examiners, of whom at least one shall be external to the Institute.

15.3.3 Following examination, the student may be required to undertake minor or major revisions, as specified by the examiners, and shall have up to six (6) months to complete major revisions.

### 15.4 Extension of Candidature
15.4.1 The maximum duration for a Master's research programme is three (3) years and for a doctoral programme is six (6) years, from the date of first registration.

15.4.2 Extensions beyond the maximum duration may be granted by the Academic Council in exceptional circumstances, on the recommendation of the supervisor and Head of Department.

## 16. Use of Institute Facilities and IT Resources

### 16.1 Library Services
16.1.1 Students may borrow up to eight (8) books at a time from the central library, for a loan period of fourteen (14) days, renewable twice provided no other student has placed a hold on the title.

16.1.2 Reference materials, rare books, and periodicals are for in-library use only and may not be borrowed.

16.1.3 Overdue fines accrue as specified in the Fee Deadlines and Penalties document.

### 16.2 Computing and Network Resources
16.2.1 Every student is issued an Institute email account and network credentials upon registration, which remain active until six (6) months after completion or withdrawal from the programme.

16.2.2 Use of Institute computing resources for commercial purposes, unauthorized distribution of copyrighted material, or any unlawful activity is prohibited and may be treated as a disciplinary matter under Section 9.

16.2.3 The Institute reserves the right to monitor network traffic on Institute infrastructure for security purposes, in accordance with the IT Usage Policy published separately.

### 16.3 Laboratory Access
16.3.1 Access to specialized laboratories outside scheduled class hours requires prior authorization from the concerned faculty member or laboratory in-charge, and adherence to the safety protocols specific to that laboratory.

16.3.2 A student who violates laboratory safety protocols may have their access privileges suspended by the laboratory in-charge, pending review by the Head of Department.

## 17. Anti-Ragging Policy

### 17.1 Prohibition
17.1.1 Ragging in any form, including verbal, physical, or psychological harassment of a student by another student or group of students, is strictly prohibited on Institute premises, in hostels, and at Institute-organized events, in accordance with applicable statutory anti-ragging regulations.

### 17.2 Reporting
17.2.1 Complaints of ragging may be made to the Anti-Ragging Committee, the Hostel Warden, the Head of Department, or through the anonymous reporting channel maintained on the Institute website.

17.2.2 All complaints of ragging shall be treated with the highest priority and shall be forwarded to the Disciplinary Committee within twenty-four (24) hours of receipt.

### 17.3 Sanctions
17.3.1 Sanctions for proven ragging may include suspension, expulsion, and reporting to law enforcement authorities where the conduct constitutes a criminal offence, in addition to any other action the Disciplinary Committee considers appropriate.

## 18. Student Societies and Extracurricular Activities

### 18.1 Recognition of Societies
18.1.1 A group of at least fifteen (15) students may apply to the Office of Student Affairs for recognition as an official Institute society, on submission of a constitution and a list of office-bearers.

18.1.2 Recognized societies are eligible to apply for funding from the Student Activities Fund, subject to the guidelines published by the Office of Student Affairs.

### 18.2 Conduct at Events
18.2.1 Societies organizing events on campus must register the event with the Office of Student Affairs at least seven (7) working days in advance.

18.2.2 The Institute reserves the right to withdraw recognition from a society found to be in persistent violation of these regulations or of the Institute's Code of Conduct.

## 19. Internship and Industry Placement

### 19.1 Mandatory Internship
19.1.1 Every B.Tech student must complete a minimum of eight (8) weeks of internship with an Institute-approved organization before the start of the final year, unless exempted under clause 19.1.3.

19.1.2 Internships must be registered with the Training and Placement Office prior to commencement, and a completion certificate along with a supervisor evaluation must be submitted within two (2) weeks of the internship's conclusion.

19.1.3 The Dean of Academic Affairs may exempt a student from the mandatory internship requirement where the student has been engaged in a substantial research project, start-up venture, or equivalent professional experience, on application supported by the Head of Department.

19.1.4 A student who fails to complete the mandatory internship requirement, and has not been granted an exemption, shall not be permitted to register for final-year coursework and shall be required to complete the internship in the intervening period before registration is allowed.

### 19.2 Placement Process
19.2.1 The Training and Placement Office coordinates campus placement activities for final-year students in their last two terms of study.

19.2.2 A student who accepts a job offer through the campus placement process and subsequently withdraws without valid reason, as determined by the Training and Placement Office, may be barred from further placement activities for that academic year.

19.2.3 Participation in the placement process does not affect a student's eligibility to pursue higher studies or external opportunities, and no student is compelled to accept a placement offer.

### 19.3 Industry-Sponsored Projects
19.3.1 Final-year students may undertake an industry-sponsored capstone project in lieu of a standard academic project, subject to approval by the Head of Department and execution of a non-disclosure agreement where required by the sponsoring organization.

19.3.2 Intellectual property arising from an industry-sponsored project is governed by the terms of the sponsorship agreement, a copy of which must be filed with the Head of Department before the project commences.

## 20. Convocation and Degree Requirements

### 20.1 Eligibility for Degree Conferral
20.1.1 A student becomes eligible for conferral of a degree upon successful completion of all credit requirements of the programme, clearance of all fee dues, and clearance of all library and hostel dues, as certified by the respective offices.

20.1.2 A student under an active disciplinary sanction that includes withholding of results, as recorded under Section 9, shall not be eligible for degree conferral until the sanction period concludes or is set aside on appeal.

### 20.2 Provisional Certificates
20.2.1 A student who has met all requirements under clause 20.1.1 but whose formal degree has not yet been conferred at a convocation ceremony may request a Provisional Certificate from the Controller of Examinations, issued within ten (10) working days of the request.

### 20.3 Convocation Attendance
20.3.1 Attendance at the convocation ceremony is optional; degrees are conferred in absentia for students who do not attend, and certificates are dispatched or made available for collection as per the process notified before each convocation.

## 21. Miscellaneous

### 21.1 Identity Cards
21.1.1 Every student must carry a valid Institute identity card at all times on campus and produce it on demand to any Institute officer.

### 21.2 Leave of Absence From the Programme
21.2.1 A student may apply for a Leave of Absence from the programme for up to two consecutive academic terms, for reasons including medical necessity, family circumstances, or participation in an approved external programme.

21.2.2 Leave of Absence must be approved by the Head of Department and does not count toward the maximum permissible duration of the programme.

21.2.3 A student on approved Leave of Absence is not liable for tuition fees during the leave period but remains liable for any hostel fees if accommodation is retained.

### 21.3 Use of Institute Facilities
21.3.1 Access to libraries, laboratories, and computing facilities outside scheduled class hours is subject to the specific operating rules of each facility, published separately by the facility in-charge.

### 21.4 Interpretation
21.4.1 Headings used in these regulations are for convenience of reference only and do not affect interpretation.

21.4.2 Where these regulations use the word "may," the relevant action is discretionary; where they use the word "shall" or "must," the relevant action is mandatory.
